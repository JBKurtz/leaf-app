With a degree in Botany and background in tech, I've long drempt of a way to bring together human knowledge about plants and make it possible to identify and learn about them effortlessly. Other plant apps have never reached the potential of what I've envisioned, so I built Leaf. Leaf uses a neural recognition model and a localized plant trait database to bring you guided observations which will help you identify any plant. I am looking for beta testers to use Leaf and hear from how the app feels to use in practice.

What I'm looking for

What I'm hoping to learn.

All feedback is welcome, in the app, through a short form, or by email. A few questions I'm especially interested in:

  • ✓ Does the camera match the right species or genus, and how often?
  • ✓ Is the step from the camera's answer to your own checks clear?
  • ✓ Does Leaf ask the right questions, and which would you cut?
  • ✓ Where do the guided observations break down or stop narrowing well?
  • ✓ Are the guided observations easy to move through?
  • ✓ Are the plant profiles compelling to read, and which pages do you use?
  • ✓ Are the glossary explanations enough when you need them?
  • ✓ Do the plant toxicity warnings and invasiveness treatments land right with you?
What Leaf supports today

What's covered.

Every trait and fact in Leaf traces to an authoritative, peer-reviewed flora, such as the Flora of North America. Over 12,500 California plants have full profiles today, more added every week. Natives are its strength; houseplants its weak spot, for now.
