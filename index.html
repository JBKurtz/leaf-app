How it works

Your camera scans the plant. Your observations finish the job.

A photo can tell an oak from a pine. It often can't tell one oak from the next. Leaf matches the scan to one or more plants, then guides your own observations to land the identification.

1

Point the camera

Frame a plant in the leaf and tap Identify. Guide the frame as Leaf scans up to three snaps, from the whole plant to a close-up, zooming in on its own.

Leaf matches the photos to the plants that grow locally to where you are.

2

Guided observations

When the photos match a genus, Leaf says so and how many of its species grow nearby. Here it's a eucalyptus, with 21 kinds in the watershed; plant photos and guided observations help you land the identification.

Leaf guides you to look for the traits that make a candidate plant stand out. Is the plant growing out from standing water? Is the fruit an acorn? Each answer drops the plants that don't fit.

Each plant has an average of 5 photos that can also help you make a confident identification.

3

You make the informed call

Open a candidate and check it against the plant before you: its size and habit, what sets it apart, its leaves, flower or fruit. Compare to photos and the plant's look-a-likes to be sure.

You decide. "This is the plant" saves it to your Field Log with the place and date; "Not this one" moves you to the next candidate.

4

Meet the plant

Every identified plant opens into a rich, encyclopedic profile including range, growing conditions, traits and human uses including its foraging and medicinal potential. The pages are below.

The profile

Identify a plant, then learn all there is to know.

Plants open into an encyclopedia of information. Over 12,500 California plants have a full profile today. Here are some pages for the Coast Live Oak.

01 · Overview

The plant at a glance

Photos, its names, whether it's native, and badges for edible, medicinal, invasive or toxic. A short description, quick facts such as height and lifespan, and what sets it apart.

02 · Identify

Telling it from its look-alikes

The nearby plants it's most often confused with, see what's different trait by trait: habit, leaf shape, margin, fruit timing, bark.

03 · Form

Every part of the plant, in plain language

What defines this plant - all the characteristics it exhibits. Its leaves, flowers, fruit and seed, stem and bark. Leaf speaks in every day terms by default. Botanical terminology and hand-lens details siting behind an advanced toggle. And a full glossary fills in any gaps.

04 · The year

Its seasons

Where is the plant in its growth cycle throughout the year? See when it flowers and when it fruits.

05 · Range and habitat

Where it grows wild

A map of where this plant grows, the habitats it favors, its climate, and its native range. Also see what plants are known to grow alongside.

06 · Uses

Materials and traditions

Its recorded uses beyond food or medicine, from firewood and timber to ceremony and games.

07 · Edible and medicinal

Food and medicine, and who used them

Time to forage? How is it prepared? What are some traditional medicinal uses that are recorded?

08 · Safety

Before you touch it or eat it

Whether it's toxic and how seriously, for people and for pets and livestock, which parts, and the signs of poisoning to recognize.

09 · Cultivate and grow

Cultivation

What does this plant need to thrive? Water, light, soil and pH, drought and shade tolerance, maintenance. See how it can be propagated and how to grow it.

10 · Related

Its family, and its neighbors

The family line from family to siblings in its genus to its varieties, the relatives that grow near you, its other names.
