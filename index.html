What Leaf does

A knowledgeable friend on the trail.

Point Leaf at what's growing around you and it tells you what it is, whether it's worth knowing, and what makes this place its own. No expertise required.

🌴

Start where you stand

Leaf reads your location and shows the plants observed in your watershed and at your elevation, so you begin from the set that actually grows where you are. That narrows the search before you even start.

👀

No camera, no guessing

Leaf doesn't point a lens and guess. You look at the plant, its leaf shape, flower color, bark, and habit, and match what you see. Each observation narrows the field, you're interacting with the plant in order to identify it. A little more effort, on purpose.

📖

Reference-grade profiles

Every plant has its own page: photos, a plain-language description, a range map, look-alikes to tell apart, its cultural significance and traditional uses, and perhaps a thing or two you didn't know.

🌿

Native, edible, medicinal

See at a glance which plants are native, which are invasive, and which have a place in food and medicine.

📍

A sense of place

Leaf also tells you the park you're in, the Indigenous people whose homeland it is, and the habitats and plant communities around you.

Grounded, not guessed

Real botany, made effortless.

Leaf pairs peer-reviewed botanical data with a little intelligence: it works out the single most useful question to ask you next, and weaves dozens of published floras into readable profiles. The knowledge stays grounded in real sources, never invented.

  • ✓ Sourced from Flora of North America, Jepson, and other public floras
  • ✓ Location-first: the plants that actually live where you are
  • ✓ Trait-driven identification that teaches you the why
  • ✓ A plain-language glossary is a long-press away
  • ✓ Your finds saved to a personal field log, no account needed
  • ✓ Identifying plants and reading their profiles is free, always
Early and growing

Honest about where we are.

Leaf is young and improving every day. It covers California, with over 8,500 native and introduced plants supported now and more added daily toward all of the roughly 23,000 you can find in the state. It is strongest on native plants, and best with trees, shrubs, vines, wildflowers, and forbs. Cacti, succulents, grasses, ferns, and mosses are not supported yet.
