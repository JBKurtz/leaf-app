What Leaf does

A knowledgeable friend on the trail.

Point Leaf at what's growing around you and it tells you what it is, whether it's worth knowing, and what makes this place its own. No expertise required.

🌴

Start where you stand

Leaf reads your location and shows the plants actually documented near you, along with the ecology and habitats of this exact spot. You choose from hundreds of local plants, not thousands.

👆

Identify by sight

Tap the traits you can see, leaf shape, flower color, bark, habit, and each choice narrows the field. It's built on the same logic a botanist uses, so it teaches you why, not just what.

📖

Reference-grade profiles

Every plant has its own page: photos, a plain-language description, a range map, look-alikes to tell apart, its cultural significance and traditional uses, and a few things you didn't know. Drawn from peer-reviewed floras, so it reads like a trustworthy field guide, not a search result.

🌿

Native, edible, medicinal

See at a glance which plants are native, which are invasive, and which have a place in food and medicine. Every label is sourced, so you can trust what you're reading.

📍

A sense of place

Leaf also tells you the park you're in, the Indigenous people whose homeland it is, and the plant communities around you. Reason enough to open it even without a plant to name.

Grounded, not guessed

Real botany, made effortless.

Leaf pairs peer-reviewed botanical data with a little intelligence: it works out the single most useful question to ask you next, and weaves dozens of published floras into readable profiles. The knowledge stays grounded in real sources, never invented.

  • ✓ Sourced from Flora of North America, Jepson, and other public floras
  • ✓ Location-first: the plants that actually live where you are
  • ✓ Trait-driven identification that teaches you the why
  • ✓ A plain-language glossary is a long-press away
  • ✓ Your finds saved to a personal field log, no account needed
Early and growing

Honest about where we are.

Leaf is young and improving quickly. Today it covers California and focuses on woody plants like trees and shrubs and herbaceous plants like wildflowers and forbs. More regions and more plant groups are on the way.
